Two ways of learning a language keep pulling against each other in your notes: Femke's daily flashcards, and the months of listening in A Year of Low Tides. ([Dutch lesson 1 with Femke](../sources/mock-2.md), [A Year of Low Tides, chapters 3 to 5](../sources/mock-8.md)) You're doing both, and since late October a Dutch podcast on the way to work too. ([A Year of Low Tides, chapters 3 to 5](../sources/mock-8.md), [Dutch lesson 5: separable verbs](../sources/mock-11.md))

## Current understanding

- Femke thinks vocabulary, not grammar, is the bottleneck for the first three months. So: 20 flashcards a day. ([Dutch lesson 1 with Femke](../sources/mock-2.md))
- 15 minutes of a Dutch news podcast a day, on the way to work. ([Dutch lesson 5: separable verbs](../sources/mock-11.md))
- In the book, Ilse Brandsma listens at the harbor for months before speaking, then starts with phrases she's heard a hundred times. ([A Year of Low Tides, chapters 3 to 5](../sources/mock-8.md)) Halfway through her year she finally gets a joke there. ([A Year of Low Tides, chapters 6 to 8](../sources/mock-12.md))

## Contradictions

- **Drills or listening.** Femke's answer is flashcards every day, and listening doesn't replace them. ([Dutch lesson 1 with Femke](../sources/mock-2.md), [Dutch lesson 5: separable verbs](../sources/mock-11.md)) Brandsma says drills teach you the words and the harbor teaches you when to say them, and deleted her word app in the first month. ([A Year of Low Tides, chapters 3 to 5](../sources/mock-8.md)) You called yourself the experiment. ([A Year of Low Tides, chapters 3 to 5](../sources/mock-8.md))
