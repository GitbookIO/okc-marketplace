Two ways of learning a language keep pulling against each other in your notes: Femke's [daily flashcards](../sources/mock-2.md), and the months of [listening in A Year of Low Tides](../sources/mock-8.md). You're doing both, and since late October a [Dutch podcast](../sources/mock-11.md) on the way to work too.

## Current understanding

- Femke thinks [vocabulary, not grammar, is the bottleneck](../sources/mock-2.md) for the first three months. So: 20 flashcards a day.
- 15 minutes of a [Dutch news podcast](../sources/mock-11.md) a day, on the way to work.
- In the book, Ilse Brandsma [listens at the harbor](../sources/mock-8.md) for months before speaking, then starts with phrases she's heard a hundred times. Halfway through her year she [finally gets a joke](../sources/mock-12.md) there.

## Contradictions

- **Drills or listening.** Femke's answer is [flashcards every day](../sources/mock-2.md), and [listening doesn't replace them](../sources/mock-11.md). [Brandsma says](../sources/mock-8.md) drills teach you the words and the harbor teaches you when to say them, and deleted her word app in the first month. You called yourself the experiment.
