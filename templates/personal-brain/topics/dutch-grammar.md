The grammar that matters most for a first real conversation: where the verb goes, which article a noun takes, how separable verbs split, which helper the past tense takes, and when to say u.[^sources-mock-2][^sources-mock-7][^sources-mock-11][^sources-mock-14] The past tense is where Maya still freezes.[^sources-mock-14]

## Current understanding

- The verb comes second in a main clause: "Morgen ga ik naar Utrecht".[^sources-mock-2]
- After dat, omdat and als, the verb goes to the end: "Ik denk dat ik morgen kom".[^sources-mock-2]
- There's no rule for de or het that works, so learn the article with the noun. Every diminutive is het: het huisje, het meisje.[^sources-mock-7]
- Separable verbs split in a main clause and stay together at the end of a subclause: "Ik bel je morgen op", "omdat ik je morgen opbel".[^sources-mock-11]
- The past tense takes zijn for moving somewhere and for changes of state, and hebben for nearly everything else: "Ik ben naar Utrecht gegaan", "Ik heb gegeten".[^sources-mock-14]
- Since 4 November: with Oma, start with u and switch when she says "zeg maar je". Many older people in Friesland expect u at first.[^sources-mock-14]

## Contradictions

- **U or je with strangers.** The textbook says u with anyone you don't know and anyone older.[^sources-mock-7] Femke says almost nobody under 60 says u anymore, and je is the default even with strangers in shops.[^sources-mock-7] Femke's later advice agrees with the textbook only for older people.[^sources-mock-14]
